CASE STUDY

CHROME: a mobile-first, WhatsApp-led marketing site for a Nairobi car wash

A portfolio project exploring how a small premium service business in Kenya can convert mobile traffic into bookings with a fast, accessible, WhatsApp-first site — no framework, no backend, no budget for custom photography.

1. The brief

Design and build a premium-feeling website for a fictional car wash in Donholm, Nairobi. The goal: increase WhatsApp bookings from mobile users in Nairobi's Eastlands — Donholm, Fedha, Tassia, Taj Mall, Tel Aviv.

Success in this market is not "a beautiful website." It's a site that turns a mobile visitor into a WhatsApp message in under 60 seconds.

2. Constraints

  • Mobile-only reality. Most users will arrive on a mid-range Android over 4G — often metered. Every KB counts.
  • WhatsApp is the channel. Kenyan SMEs do not book through email or web forms. WhatsApp is the primary business tool.
  • M-Pesa is payment, not a channel. It happens after the service, in person. Designing for it is a UX non-goal.
  • No brand assets. No logo, no photography, no color system. Everything had to be decided or borrowed from a design system.
  • No backend. A static site, or nothing.

3. Research & insights

Three assumptions shaped every decision. These are drawn from observing how mid-market Kenyan service businesses actually convert.

Insight 1 — Trust is the actual product

Car owners' #1 fear is not price. It's damage. That's why the site repeatedly names "two-bucket hand wash", "pH-neutral products", and "no machine brushes" — the language of craft that signals safety. The testimonial column is not decoration; it is the conversion engine.

Insight 2 — Speed-of-reply beats speed-of-page

Users don't care if the site loads in 1s or 3s — they care if the WhatsApp reply comes in 5 minutes. The site promises this explicitly ("we'll confirm within 5 minutes") because it is a competitive advantage, not a feature.

Insight 3 — Local language builds trust cheaply

"Safi kabisa" and "Hapa ni kwenu. Karibu CHROME." are used sparingly, in high-trust moments (testimonials, closing CTAs). The rest of the site is English. Mixing registers deliberately signals: we are professional, and we are yours.

4. Design decisions

Why graphite + gold

The dark canvas gives the site a "premium studio" feel — a small visual claim to justify premium pricing in a category where most competitors look identical (yellow-and-blue). Gold is the accent — reserved for CTAs — and provides contrast without a red/orange "discount" signal.

Why "Book" is "Book via WhatsApp"

"Book Now" is a web-native phrase. It implies a form. In Kenya, booking is a conversation. Labelling the CTA "Book via WhatsApp" reduces the cognitive step from "click" to "chat" — the user already knows what happens.

Why the page reads like a sales argument

Most premium service sites start with a hero image and a brand promise. CHROME starts with the customer's pain: "You've been to car washes that scratch paint, miss spots, and make you wait 2 hours." The hero earns attention with a specific claim ("Your car, spotless in 45 minutes — without the stress"). Every section answers a real objection.

Typography

Fraunces (a "soft serif") signals craft and human quality. Manrope keeps the interface clean and legible on cheap Android screens. Two families only — no display-font stacking.

Motion with restraint

Hero title uses a staggered reveal (once, on load). Cards use scroll-triggered fades. Nothing autoplays, nothing parallaxes. The point is that the site feels alive without being busy. Everything degrades gracefully under prefers-reduced-motion.

5. Technical decisions

Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS — no framework

A 10-page marketing site does not need React. Next.js would add 60–90KB of JS to ship what is essentially static HTML. Vanilla wins on every axis that matters here: initial load, time-to-interactive, hosting, maintainability, and hire-ability (it shows you can build without a scaffold).

WhatsApp handoff instead of a form backend

The contact form and the booking flow both encode their state into a pre-filled wa.me link. This gives the business a real inbox (WhatsApp Business) with no server, no spam mitigation, and a native mobile experience for the operator.

Progressive Web App

A service worker caches the app shell, so the site loads near-instantly on repeat visits — a meaningful advantage in a market where users return frequently to check hours or book again. An install prompt lets them pin CHROME to their home screen.

View Transitions API

Where supported, navigation between pages cross-fades with a subtle vertical motion. Zero JS, zero library. Progressive enhancement: no support, no problem.

Accessibility as a gate, not a checkbox

Contrast pairs (gold-on-dark, white-on-graphite) were checked before shipping. Every interactive element has a visible focus state. FAQ accordions use real aria-expanded. The mobile menu restores focus. Reduced motion is respected throughout.

Images

Hero images are preloaded with fetchpriority="high" and served as responsive srcset sets from Unsplash. Content images are lazy-loaded with fixed aspect ratios so they never shift layout. Placeholder photography is used deliberately — a real deployment would replace these with the studio's own work.

What I'd measure next, if this were live: WhatsApp click-through rate on mobile (target 8–12% from the homepage), install rate for the PWA, repeat-visit rate via "Book again" banner impressions, and the LCP on a throttled 3G connection.

7. What I'd do with a real budget

  • Custom photography. Three hours of a photographer at the studio would outperform every stock image on this site.
  • WhatsApp Business API. Automated confirmation replies and a live queue widget ("3 cars ahead of you").
  • A CMS. Today the customer would have to call a developer to update prices. Astro or 11ty with a lightweight CMS would fix this.
  • Bilingual EN/SW toggle. Deferred here to keep the piece focused, but the market clearly justifies it.
  • Real analytics. Umami or Plausible, self-hosted, without third-party scripts or cookies.
